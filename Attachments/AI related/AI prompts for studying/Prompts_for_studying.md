# Non-checked, initial direction prompts
1. Spaced Repetition

“Create a spaced-repetition plan for the topic [Topic]. Turn the key ideas into short question-and-answer flashcards and distribute them according to a schedule: day 1, day 3, day 7, day 14, and day 30. Prioritize things that are easy to forget or frequently confused.”

2. Start by Learning the Most Important 20%

“I need to quickly master [Topic]. Identify the 20% of concepts that provide approximately 80% of practical understanding. Build a 20-hour plan from them, divided into 10 sessions. For each session, specify: what to study, what to practise, one useful resource, and a short exercise for reinforcement.”

3. Test Yourself While You Don't Really Understand

“Ask me 10 questions about [Topic], gradually increasing in difficulty. One at a time. After each answer, evaluate it, explain what I understood correctly and what I didn’t, and identify the specific gap in my understanding. At the end, give me a short revision plan focused on my weakest areas.”

4. Use the Feynman Method

“Explain [Topic] to me in the simplest language possible. Then ask me to explain it back in my own words, as if I were explaining it to a beginner. Identify what I misunderstood, what I missed, and where my explanation was vague. Re-explain only the weak areas and make me explain them again — until I can do it clearly and without hints.”

5. Make a 5-Minute Cheat Sheet

“Turn [Topic] into a one-page cheat sheet that can be read in 5 minutes. Include key definitions, concepts, formulas, rules, typical mistakes, simple examples, and connections between them. Keep everything compact and structured so that the most important information can be grasped at a glance.”

6. SHOW ME MY BLIND SPOTS

"I think I understand [topic] fairly well. Test this: give me 7 deceptively simple questions that expose weak foundations, misconceptions, and missing connections. After each answer, explain what it reveals about me and tell me exactly what I need to review."

7. TURN THEORY INTO REAL-WORLD TASKS

"Teach me [topic] through real-world situations, not through bare theory. Give me 5 practical scenarios where I would actually have to apply it. First let me solve it myself, then explain what I did well, what I missed, and how an experienced professional would approach it."

8. BUILD ME A LEARNING LADDER

"Break down [topic] into 5 levels of difficulty — from complete beginner to advanced. For each level, tell me: what I should understand, what I should be able to do, one practical assignment, and the signs that I'm ready to move on."

9. FIND ONLY THE BEST MATERIALS

"Find the 5 highest‑quality sources for learning [topic] — books, courses, videos, websites, or specific experts.
Rank them from the most useful to the least useful for a beginner. For each one, explain: what it teaches best, who it's suitable for, how much time it will take, and why it deserves my attention."
